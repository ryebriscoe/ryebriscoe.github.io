About me

I broke my mom's MacBook when I was twelve. It had stopped charging, so I spent hours watching repair videos and then went at the charger myself, stripping it back and holding wires together until the light came on. It worked for about five minutes. Then it sparked and I short-circuited the whole computer.

I thought that would put me off. It did the opposite. I wanted to know exactly what I had done wrong. I started building PCs after that, and by thirteen I was digging into networks and running Wireshark, mostly because I wanted to understand the games I was playing and figure out what an IP address actually was.

I played NCAA basketball at CSU Monterey Bay while working through this degree. A season and a full CS course load at the same time meant I had to get good at planning around deadlines I could not move. Practice, travel, and road games do not care what is due on Friday. That is probably the part of school that changed how I work the most.

Basketball also ended up feeding my projects. I am building B.A.D., a system that watches game film and identifies the offensive play being run. Knowing the sport from the inside is what lets me judge whether the model is right. Alongside that I am working through certifications in AI engineering and project management, and I graduate in {{ site.school.graduation }}.

The program

The CS Online program at CSUMB covers the core of computer science and then pushes it into practice. The coursework is project based, so most classes end with something you built rather than something you memorized. Everyone starts with a shared foundation in programming, systems, and theory, then moves into a concentration. Mine is Software Engineering. The degree closes with a capstone that pulls the whole thing into one real project.

Goals

I want to work as a software engineer on systems where machine learning is one piece of something larger rather than the whole product. B.A.D. is the clearest example of what I mean. There is a vision model in it, but most of the actual work is tracking, handling data, and building an honest way to measure whether the output is any good.

The certifications cover what the coursework does not. IBM AI Engineering and Generative AI with Large Language Models go deeper on the model side than my classes did. Google Project Management is for the other half of the job, which is running work that involves other people. I want to finish this degree with real projects behind me and not just a transcript.
