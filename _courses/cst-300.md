A research paper on using drones for disaster response and humanitarian aid, drawing on eight peer reviewed and institutional sources.

The argument starts from a hard number: rescue teams can take days to reach survivors, and the first 72 hours are the ones that decide outcomes. Drones close that gap. But the paper does not stop at the upside. It takes on aerial surveillance of people who never consented to being filmed, uncoordinated drone traffic obstructing the rescues it is meant to help, and the fact that this technology reaches wealthy countries first while the communities that need it most go without.
